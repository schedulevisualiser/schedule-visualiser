Questions

Do I need a P6 licence?

No. You need someone on the project to send you a .xer export — after that, everything happens in your browser. That's the point: the people with questions about the schedule usually aren't the people sitting in front of P6 all day.

What is the WBS network map?

P6 shows the WBS as a tree of bars. This shows it as a map — one node per WBS element, with the logic that runs between them — so you can see how the work flows across disciplines and where the critical path crosses between them. Expand any node to drill into the activities underneath, right down to individual tasks.

What is "driving logic", and why does it matter?

A relationship is driving when the predecessor is genuinely holding the successor's date, rather than just being connected to it. Tracing all logic answers "what is this linked to?"; following the driving logic answers "what is actually pushing this date?" — usually the question you meant. P6 calls it the driving path.

Does it change or re-calculate my schedule?

No — it's read-only, and it deliberately trusts P6's own calculated dates and total float rather than re-running its own CPM. P6 stays the scheduling engine. Critical path analysis uses P6's total float, so what you see here agrees with what P6 says.

Where does my schedule data go?

Nowhere. The file is read locally by your browser and is never sent to any server. There's no upload step, no account, and nothing stored online.

What files can I open?

Primavera P6 .xer exports, including files containing several projects — you pick which one to view. Support for P6 XML is on the list.

How big a schedule can it handle?

Large ones are fine to load. The WBS network map exists precisely so that a schedule with thousands of activities opens as a handful of readable nodes rather than an unusable wall — you trace and filter down from there.

Does it work on a phone?

It's designed for a desktop or laptop screen — network maps need room. A tablet works at a push, but a phone won't give you the space to read a logic chain.
